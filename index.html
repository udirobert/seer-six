One protocol, six readings

Seer is a prediction market protocol built on Reality.eth and the Conditional Tokens Framework, which is a polite way of saying its data is a tree: a protocol root, five applications, parent markets that split into conditional children, and a dollar of collateral that becomes four outcome tokens. This entry takes that structure seriously as an interface. Every transition between these six pages is keyed to a relation that already exists in the API, and every one of them is documented, timed from a five-step scale, and — the part worth testing — still true with the motion switched off.

      The argument, in three sentences

      Animated transitions between two views of changing data measurably improve a reader's ability to track which object changed and by how much, compared with a static cut — that is Heer and Robertson's 2007 result, and it is the whole reason a market's price belongs in a transition rather than in a table cell. The same literature is equally clear about the boundary: animation that is decorative splits attention and stops helping, which is Kim, Tversky and Morrison's finding and the reason this entry publishes a contract instead of a style guide.

      So the rule every page obeys is narrow and checkable: no animation runs here unless the relation it expresses exists in Seer's data or in a Conditional Tokens Framework call. A row becoming a market document is the same object crossing a boundary. A document fanning outward is a parent collection resolving into its children. A collapse downward is redeemPositions. A 200 ms one-shot is a verification state, and it never loops, because a state is not a story.

      The third claim is the one to attack: press R on any page, or turn on reduce-motion in your operating system, and every transition resolves to its end state at zero duration rather than pausing halfway — while the depth rail, the outcome index, the signed price delta and the parent indentation stay on screen carrying the same meaning. That is a falsifiable statement, and the harness that checks it is linked below.

      • Heer, J. & Robertson, G. G. Animated Transitions in Statistical Data Graphics. IEEE Trans. Visualization & Computer Graphics 13(6), 1240–1247. The comprehension result this entry is built on.
      • Kim, S., Yoon, C., Whang, S. & Tversky, B. The effect of animation on comprehension. Int. J. Human-Computer Studies 65. The boundary condition: decorative animation splits attention. Every duration here is paid for by a relation.
      • Willenskomer, I. UX in Motion Manifesto. Continuity, relationship and expectation named as usability functions rather than taste.
      • Seer protocol documentation and public API, app.seer.pm/.netlify/functions, plus the Conditional Tokens Framework event log. Every field name on every page is quoted from that surface, not paraphrased.

      Departures, stated

      The Electric Foundation kit specifies PP Neue Montreal for display and Inter Tight for numerals. Both are commercial faces that cannot be web-delivered from a static folder without a licence, so this entry ships self-hosted IBM Plex Sans and IBM Plex Mono under the SIL Open Font License, with the licensed names kept first in every stack so the substitution is the only thing a reader without the licence can see — and the licence text shipped beside the fonts at fonts/OFL.txt. Colours, radii, spacing and the five-step duration scale are in one file, tokens.css, and no stylesheet outside it names a hue.

      Three colour departures, each one measured before it was made. The kit’s semantic hues — #2FD4B5, #FFB020 — are specified for fills, rules and marks; set as type on paper they measure 1.87 and 1.83, so text carries an ink of the same hue instead (6.06, 6.85) while the bright value stays the signal. On the dark ground the kit’s alt violet is 4.47 at 11 px and 4.13 on a raised panel, so reading violet is eight steps lighter at the same hue (5.45, 5.04) — and the violet is untouched wherever it is a mark, because a graphic owes three to one and clears it. Ink on a violet fill goes to paper: the page ground on #7D33FF is 3.52, paper is 5.54.

      None of these are preferences. Every figure here is produced by tools/a11y.mjs against the deployed address and republished on the contract, where the failing readings are printed rather than hidden.
